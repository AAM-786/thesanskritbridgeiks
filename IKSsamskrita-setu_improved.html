श्रवण-विश्लेषण · Why the system matters

Order as a Teaching Tool

How a 2,500-year-old sound classification still shapes pronunciation training, language pedagogy and modern speech technology.

०१ · Pronunciation

Muscle memory by place

Grouping sounds by where they're produced — throat, palate, roof, teeth, lips — lets a learner move through one articulator at a time (क ख ग घ ङ, all guttural) instead of memorising 33 unrelated symbols. The chart becomes a map of the mouth, not just an alphabet.

०२ · Language learning

Pattern before vocabulary

Because Pāṇini's grammar itself is built on this sound order (the Śiva Sūtras), learning the varga structure early makes later grammar — sandhi rules, verb classes, declensions — feel like consequences of sound, not arbitrary rules to memorise.

०३ · Speech technology

A ready-made phoneme feature set

Place + manner + voicing + aspiration is exactly the feature space modern ASR/TTS phoneme inventories use. Sanskrit's traditional chart gives speech systems clean, mutually exclusive articulatory classes — a natural fit for building recognisers and synthesisers for Sanskrit and its daughter languages.
